I hold a Bachelor's in Architectural Engineering and am completing a Master's in Design for Built Environment at Federico II University in Naples — while building iOS apps at the Apple Developer Academy.

My practice lives at the intersection of spatial thinking, technology, and human experience — where a floor plan and an app screen ask the same fundamental question: how do you make someone feel at home?

The architecture training is the quiet force behind everything. Designing a building teaches you to think in sequences — how someone enters, what they notice first, what they discover last. That same instinct shapes every screen I design.

The part I enjoy most is research and UX design — understanding people before designing anything for them. Watching a user do something you never expected is worth ten beautiful mockups, and it's changed the direction of almost every project I've worked on.

"The most effective design is always felt before it's understood."

GraBeat
GraBeat
UI/UX Designer · App Icon Designer
2 months · Team of 6
Pinch. Play. Repeat.
🏆 #2 App Store · Top Free Apps

A multiplayer rhythm game built on hand-tracking and music. Players use pinch gestures to catch flying notes in sync with the beat — competing to outscore each other or collaborating to keep a shared Groove Meter alive. Special note mechanics introduce strategic twists that can flip the outcome of any round.

Process In a team of six, I owned the visual language, UI system, and app icon. Playtesting exposed our core problem early: Competitive and Co-op felt interchangeable — neither delivered its intended emotion. Competitive needed tension, Co-op needed solidarity, and one visual system had to carry both. The answer wasn't separate UI skins; it was making the distinction live in the motion and colour of the notes themselves. Players now learn the game's language in their first round, no tutorial needed. Next time, I'd involve players earlier in defining what "winning together" feels like — that feedback arrived late and cost us iteration time.

02
Spottit!
Spottit!
Project Manager · UI/UX Designer
1 month · Team of 5
Check the room before you walk in.

Before you commit to going somewhere, Spottit! tells you what's actually happening there. Live space activity — Quiet, Crowded, or Collab in progress — synced instantly via CloudKit across every device. No refresh. No guessing.

Process I wore two hats here — project manager and sole UI designer in a team of five — which meant deciding fast and defending tradeoffs in the same breath. Early prototypes revealed a habit we had to unlearn in our users: they kept pull-to-refreshing space statuses like a social feed instead of trusting the live sync. The design job became making CloudKit's speed feel obvious without adding noise. The move that made it work: collapsing every possible space state into three words — Quiet, Crowded, Collab — readable at a glance, no legend. My one regret is testing mostly in controlled settings, not the chaotic rooms the app was built for.

03
Shift
Shift
UI Designer · App Icon Designer
2–3 weeks · Team of 5
Your body called. Answer it.

Pick your window — 30 seconds to five minutes. Seated or on your feet. Shift selects the exercises, times your reps, and keeps you moving without breaking your focus. You'll feel the difference by the third one.

Process One insight shaped this entire design: people dismiss break apps the moment they add cognitive load. We saw it repeatedly, testing with people in real work settings. So my job — UI and app icon, in a team of five — became subtraction: stripping out anything that made a two-minute break feel like a commitment. Watching users hesitate over ambiguous options gave us the seated-vs-standing split, and that single choice on the opening screen now personalises the whole session with zero onboarding. Looking back, I'd commit to the calm, ambient aesthetic even harder from day one — that's what actually brought people back.

04
Pass the Plot
Pass the Plot
UX Designer · Researcher · Co-Team Manager
1 month · Team of 5
Nobody remembers how it started.

A local multiplayer game where stories spiral gloriously out of control. Write a prompt. Pass it. Someone draws it. Someone else writes what they see. Repeat until the chain is unrecognisable and everyone's crying laughing. No accounts, no internet, no servers — just your devices and the inevitable chaos.

Process Our research sessions surprised us: a meaningful share of our target players had reading and writing challenges, with dyslexia coming up more than once. As co-team-manager and UX lead, I turned that finding into the product's identity — OpenDyslexic became a built-in font option, and accessibility became the story. The design tension was deliberate chaos: the game needed to spiral, never crash. Going fully offline and peer-to-peer — no accounts, no servers — earned instant trust, and trusting players write bolder prompts. My honest critique: accessibility arrived mid-project. It should have been in the first sprint.

UPP!
UPP!
UI Designer · App Flow Designer · UX Researcher
2–3 weeks · Team of 6
Designed around you, not your condition.

Smart reminders and micro-exercises designed for people living with narcolepsy. Track your energy patterns, customise your alerts, and build a day that works around your condition — not against it.

Process We couldn't interview narcolepsy patients directly, so I built our understanding from medical literature, community forums, and reviews written by people living with chronic sleep conditions. One frustration surfaced everywhere: health apps treat users as patients, prescribing routines instead of adapting to them. That became the design direction — supportive, never clinical; empowering, never diagnostic. It's why the notification system is fully customisable: users define their own alert patterns rather than following presets. What I still want for this project is one real conversation with someone living with narcolepsy. Secondary research took us far, but it isn't the same.

Huzzah
Huzzah
UI Designer · Game UI Frames · App Icon Designer
2–3 weeks · Team of 5
Your body is the weapon.

Two-player AR fantasy combat on iPadOS. Apple's Vision framework maps your body in real time — your stance becomes your defense, your gesture becomes the strike. Pick your class — Knight, Wizard, or Fairy — and step into the arena.

Process Designing UI for a game with no fixed screen: every element had to survive being overlaid on two people actively fighting, in a room the app had never seen. Health bars and class indicators kept getting obscured by arm movements, so we pushed critical information to the edges and corners, where it stayed legible through combat. I owned the full UI system — frames, character cards, app icon — and made the selection cards feel like collectibles you pull from a pack, not app buttons. The unfinished thought: the calibration screen. The moment the app first "sees" you should feel like magic, not setup.
